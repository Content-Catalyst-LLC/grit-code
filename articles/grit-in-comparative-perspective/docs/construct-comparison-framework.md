# Construct Comparison Framework

## Article

**Grit in Comparative Perspective**

## Professional comparison questions

### Grit versus self-control

- Is the challenge short-term temptation or long-term goal pursuit?
- Is the person struggling with distraction, impulse regulation, or durable commitment?

### Grit versus conscientiousness

- Is grit adding explanatory value beyond a broad personality trait?
- Are perseverance and consistency being measured distinctly?

### Grit versus resilience

- Is the issue sustained goal pursuit or recovery after adversity?
- Does continued effort support recovery, or does it increase strain?

### Grit versus deliberate practice

- Is effort merely sustained, or is it structured for improvement?
- Is feedback available and usable?

### Grit versus motivation

- Is persistence driven by autonomous values, intrinsic interest, external pressure, fear, shame, or necessity?

### Grit versus purpose

- Does the goal matter deeply enough to deserve long-term effort?
- Is persistence aligned with meaning and contribution?

### Grit versus environmental support

- Does the setting make persistence feasible, fair, and recoverable?
- Is low persistence being misread as low character when support is absent?

## Comparison table

| Construct | Best used when asking | Professional caution |
|---|---|---|
| Grit | Can the person sustain effort toward long-term goals? | Do not treat persistence as always good. |
| Self-control | Can the person regulate short-term impulses? | Do not reduce self-control to willpower. |
| Conscientiousness | Is there a broad pattern of responsibility and industriousness? | Do not ignore overlap with grit. |
| Resilience | Can the person recover after adversity? | Do not romanticize adversity. |
| Deliberate practice | Is effort structured for skill growth? | Do not assume more effort means better practice. |
| Purpose | Does the goal carry meaning? | Do not turn purpose into self-erasure. |
| Environmental support | Does the situation support sustained effort? | Do not blame people for poor design. |

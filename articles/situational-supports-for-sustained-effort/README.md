# Situational Supports for Sustained Effort

This article folder supports reproducible research examples for **Situational Supports for Sustained Effort** in the Grit knowledge series.

The scaffold is designed for professional positive psychologists, psychometricians, educational researchers, organizational psychologists, program evaluators, and applied researchers who need transparent materials for studying sustained effort as a person-environment process.

## Research focus

This article examines the situational conditions that support adaptive persistence, including:

- autonomy support
- competence support
- feedback quality
- belonging
- relatedness
- mentoring access
- psychological safety
- material resources
- recovery capacity
- workload realism
- fairness
- perceived opportunity
- chronic stress
- blocked opportunity
- burnout risk
- goal progress
- wellbeing
- adaptive persistence

## Professional framing

Sustained effort should not be interpreted as an isolated character trait. A professional positive-psychology account should examine how environments cultivate, drain, distort, or sustain effort.

This repository is designed to help researchers and practitioners evaluate whether settings make persistence possible, meaningful, humane, and developmentally appropriate.

## Repository purpose

This scaffold is designed for synthetic-data demonstration, research education, article support, reproducible workflow design, professional interpretation training, and positive psychology program-evaluation examples.

It is not a psychological test, screening tool, diagnostic system, hiring tool, admissions tool, student-ranking system, employee-evaluation system, or individual assessment instrument.

## Folder structure

- `docs/` — construct definition, measurement notes, ethics, limitations, and responsible-use notes
- `data/raw/` — synthetic situational-support survey data and data dictionary
- `data/processed/` — generated modeled datasets
- `python/` — support modeling, psychometrics, moderation, and burnout-safety workflows
- `r/` — parallel positive psychology workflows
- `sql/` — research schema and analytical views
- `julia/` — compact situational-support simulation
- `c/`, `cpp/`, `fortran/`, `go`, `rust` — small computational examples
- `notebooks/` — professional notebook plan
- `outputs/tables/` — generated tables
- `outputs/figures/` — generated figures

## Professional caution

Situational-support research should not be used to excuse low expectations or to erase individual agency. It should also not be used to blame individuals for outcomes shaped by poor instruction, unsafe institutions, discrimination, inaccessible systems, unsupported caregiving, poverty, workload overload, or lack of opportunity.

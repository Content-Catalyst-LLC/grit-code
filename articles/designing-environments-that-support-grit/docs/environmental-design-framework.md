# Environmental Design Framework

## Article

**Designing Environments That Support Grit**

## Design domains

### 1. Autonomy support

Design questions:

- Do people understand why the goal matters?
- Do they have voice in method, pacing, strategy, or revision?
- Are they treated as agents rather than compliance objects?

### 2. Competence support

Design questions:

- Are standards clear?
- Is feedback actionable?
- Are practice and revision built into the environment?
- Can people see progress?

### 3. Belonging and relational support

Design questions:

- Do people feel recognized and included?
- Do they have mentors or peers?
- Is struggle normalized as part of development?

### 4. Psychological safety

Design questions:

- Can people ask questions?
- Can they admit mistakes?
- Can they seek help without humiliation?

### 5. Recovery design

Design questions:

- Is workload realistic?
- Is recovery protected?
- Are boundaries respected?
- Is exhaustion treated as a design signal?

### 6. Material access

Design questions:

- Do people have the time, tools, transportation, technology, financial support, care support, health access, or accommodations needed to persist?

### 7. Fairness and pathway credibility

Design questions:

- Are standards transparent?
- Are opportunities real?
- Is evaluation fair?
- Are second chances available?

### 8. Adaptive quitting and revision

Design questions:

- Can people revise goals without shame?
- Are exit pathways dignified?
- Does the environment distinguish challenge from harm?

## Professional caution

An environment can look demanding while being poorly designed. Grit-supportive design requires both standards and support.

# Methods Note

This article folder demonstrates how grit and narrative identity can be modeled as related but distinct contributors to long-term persistence.

A simple long-term persistence model can include grit, narrative coherence, agency, purpose, support, and burnout:

\[
L_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1G_i + \beta_2N_i + \beta_3A_i + \beta_4P_i + \beta_5S_i - \beta_6B_i + \epsilon_i
\]

where \(L_i\) represents long-term persistence, \(G_i\) is grit, \(N_i\) is narrative coherence, \(A_i\) is agency, \(P_i\) is purpose or future orientation, \(S_i\) is support, and \(B_i\) is burnout.

Narrative identity can be represented as a composite of coherence, agency, meaning-making, and future orientation:

\[
N_i = w_C C_i + w_A A_i + w_M M_i + w_F F_i
\]

where \(N_i\) represents narrative identity strength or integration, \(C_i\) is coherence, \(A_i\) is agency, \(M_i\) is meaning-making, \(F_i\) is future orientation, and the weights represent their relative contribution.

Narrative identity may also moderate the relationship between grit and persistence:

\[
L_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1G_i + \beta_2N_i + \beta_3(G_i \times N_i) + \epsilon_i
\]

The workflows use synthetic data to show that narrative identity may help connect grit to coherence, agency, meaning-making, and future orientation, while support, feedback, opportunity, institutional trust, health, burnout, and narrative strain shape whether effort remains adaptive.

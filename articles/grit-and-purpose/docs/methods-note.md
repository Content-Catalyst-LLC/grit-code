# Methods Note

This article folder demonstrates how grit and purpose can be modeled as related but distinct contributors to long-term persistence.

A simple long-term persistence model can include grit, purpose, support, feedback, and burnout:

\[
L_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1G_i + \beta_2U_i + \beta_3S_i + \beta_4F_i - \beta_5B_i + \epsilon_i
\]

where \(L_i\) represents long-term persistence, \(G_i\) is grit, \(U_i\) is purpose, \(S_i\) is support, \(F_i\) is feedback quality, and \(B_i\) is burnout.

Purpose can be represented as a composite of meaning, direction, and beyond-the-self contribution:

\[
U_i = w_M M_i + w_D D_i + w_C C_i
\]

where \(U_i\) represents purpose, \(M_i\) is personal meaning, \(D_i\) is long-term direction, \(C_i\) is beyond-the-self contribution, and the weights represent their relative importance.

Purpose may also moderate the relationship between grit and persistence:

\[
L_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1G_i + \beta_2U_i + \beta_3(G_i \times U_i) + \epsilon_i
\]

The workflows use synthetic data to show that purpose can give grit direction and meaning, while support, feedback, autonomy, opportunity, health, and burnout shape whether purposeful persistence remains sustainable.

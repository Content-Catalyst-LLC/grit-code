# Construct Definition

## Article

**Can Grit Be Taught?**

## Primary construct

This article examines the teachability of grit through a professional positive-psychology lens. Grit should be treated as a construct involving sustained effort and relatively stable commitment to long-term goals, but its development depends on context, feedback, support, recovery, opportunity, and purpose.

## Core constructs

### Grit

A broad construct often defined through perseverance and passion for long-term goals. In professional interpretation, grit should not be reduced to toughness, compliance, productivity, or refusal to quit.

### Perseverance of effort

Sustained effort in the face of difficulty, delay, boredom, or setbacks.

### Consistency of interests

Relative stability of long-term interests or goals. This should be interpreted developmentally because exploration can be healthy, especially in adolescence and emerging adulthood.

### Adaptive persistence

Sustained effort that remains responsive to feedback, health, autonomy, recovery, purpose, and changing evidence.

### Grit-support intervention

A structured program intended to support adaptive persistence through goal clarity, practice design, feedback literacy, recovery planning, self-regulation, social support, purpose reflection, and adaptive quitting.

## Construct boundaries

Grit should not be conflated with:

- moral worth
- achievement
- self-control alone
- conscientiousness as a whole
- obedience
- productivity
- resilience after trauma
- willingness to endure exploitation
- absence of distress
- refusal to quit under all circumstances

## Professional interpretation

A claim that grit can be taught should specify what changed, how it changed, for whom it changed, under what conditions it changed, and whether wellbeing and burnout outcomes were monitored.
